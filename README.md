# Gasketed-Plate Heat Exchanger: Design, Simulation, and Prototype Fabrication

It is a Level 3 Term 2 project at BUET focused on the **thermal design, numerical modeling, CAD development, and fabrication of a gasketed plate heat exchanger** for a food-processing application.

The project combined a custom MATLAB-based computational model with SolidWorks design and fabrication of a physical prototype.

## Project Overview

The objective was to design a plate heat exchanger for cooling a hot-water stream using cold water, while analyzing both its thermal performance and hydraulic behavior.

A MATLAB algorithm was developed from scratch for the design and rating calculations, without relying on commercial heat-exchanger simulation software.

## Numerical Modeling

The MATLAB model incorporated:

* Effective-NTU method for thermal performance analysis
* Newton–Raphson method for iterative calculations
* Fluid-property interpolation at different temperatures
* Reynolds number calculation and flow-regime identification
* Flow-regime-dependent Nusselt number correlations
* Friction-factor calculations for pressure-drop estimation
* Thermal and hydraulic performance calculations

A parametric study was also performed by varying:

* Number of plates
* Plate spacing
* Mass flow rate
* Plate thermal conductivity

The effects of these parameters on heat-transfer rate and pressure drop were then analyzed.

## CAD & Fabrication

The heat exchanger was modeled in SolidWorks, including the plates, gaskets, frame components, and overall assembly.

The physical prototype was fabricated using:

* Corrugated aluminum sheets
* Rubber gaskets
* Mild-steel frame
* Stud bolts
* Connecting pipes

The fabrication involved sheet-metal cutting, drilling, grinding, and arc welding.

## Testing

A hydraulic pressure test was performed on the fabricated prototype using a hand pump to examine the sealing performance of the assembly.

The prototype showed minor leakage from the gasket and welded joints at higher test pressures. The fabrication was also affected by the limited availability of CNC facilities locally, which resulted in single-sided rather than double-sided plate corrugation.
